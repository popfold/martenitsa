A Bulgarian legend

The first martenitsa

How a white thread turned red, told in a pop-up book of cross-stitch.

Open the book

Scroll to open the book and turn its pages: each one is sewn as it opens. Click a dot for more of the story.

Page one

The bundle of arrows

Khan Kubrat told his sons to stay together, like a bundle of arrows no one can break. When he died, his son Asparuh led his people south to find a new home.

Turn the page

Page two

Huba waits

Asparuh's sister Huba was held far from her brother. She waited for word that he had found the new land, so she could escape and follow him.

Page three

A falcon over the Danube

Asparuh found his land south of the Danube. He sent a falcon to Huba with a white thread tied to its leg: the sign that it was time.

Page four

White turned red

Huba escaped and set out with the falcon. An arrow struck the bird on the way, and its blood turned part of the white thread red. It reached Asparuh all the same.

The end

The first martenitsa

Asparuh tore the red and white thread into pieces and tied them to his warriors' wrists. Bulgarians still tie red and white threads, martenitsi, on the first of March, for health and for spring.

The legend has several versions: in some the bird is a swallow, or a dove, and the thread is sent by Asparuh's wife. The pictures were made with Midjourney.
